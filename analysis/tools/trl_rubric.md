# Rubric: technology and artistic readiness of a publication

Judge the work the publication reports, from its title, venue and abstract. Judge what the
publication shows, not what the authors hope for.

## TRL, technology readiness level (1–9), adapted to music technology

- 1: Basic principles observed. Theory, concepts, aesthetics, history, philosophy, surveys of ideas.
- 2: Technology concept formulated. A proposed design, model or method, not yet built or tested.
- 3: Experimental proof of concept. A first prototype, algorithm or study shows that the idea works in the lab.
- 4: Technology validated in the lab. A working system or instrument evaluated in controlled conditions (user study, measurements).
- 5: Validated in a relevant environment. Tested with real musicians, in rehearsal or a realistic setting.
- 6: Demonstrated in a relevant environment. A complete prototype used in a public performance, installation or workshop.
- 7: System prototype in an operational environment. Used repeatedly in concerts, teaching or by others outside the lab.
- 8: System complete and qualified. A stable, documented, released tool or instrument (open-source release, product).
- 9: Proven in operational use. Widely adopted, commercial, or a standard (for example OSC, Max, SuperCollider).

TRL band: 1–3 fundamental, 4–6 applied, 7–9 industrial.
Use null only when no technology is involved at all (for example a musicological study of a score).

## ARL, artistic readiness level (1–9), Jensenius's scale

- 1: Initial artistic impulse. Themes, questions or motivations identified.
- 2: Artistic concept formulated. A direction emerges; form and medium still open.
- 3: Exploratory studies. Sketches, studies or small tests show a possible artistic approach.
- 4: Artistic language validated in the studio. Methods, materials and aesthetics become coherent.
- 5: Work validated in a relevant context. Drafts tested with peers or in controlled public settings,
  such as a lab concert, an open rehearsal or a workshop showing.
- 6: Prototype artwork demonstrated. A near-complete work presented to peers in a context like its
  intended one, such as a concert, installation or demo at an academic conference.
- 7: Artistic work presented in a professional artistic context. A complete work performed,
  exhibited or released for its intended audience: a festival, concert series, gallery, theatre
  or label.
- 8: Artistic work consolidated and reproducible. Re-staged, toured, adapted or scaled.
- 9: Artistic impact demonstrated. Measurable cultural, educational or professional influence over time.

ARL band: 1–3 exploration, 4–6 development, 7–9 dissemination.
Use null when the publication has no artistic component (for example a sensor evaluation, a
latency measurement or a signal-processing method).

A conference concert or installation (NIME, ICMC, SMC and the like) is a peer setting, so a work
shown there is usually ARL 5 or 6: 6 when it is presented as a complete piece, 5 when it is a
study, a demo or a work in progress. Only the text decides a higher level: ARL 7 needs a stated
presentation in a professional artistic context (a premiere at a festival, a concert series, a
gallery, a release), and ARL 8 a work that has been toured, re-staged or adapted. A conference
co-hosted by a festival does not by itself make a work ARL 7. The same holds for papers: a paper
that describes a performance is ARL 7 only when the text places it in a professional context.

## Confidence

high: the abstract states what was built, tested or performed. medium: inferred from a short or
partial abstract. low: title only, or the text is ambiguous.
